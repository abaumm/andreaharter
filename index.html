How I work

Where I actually spend my time

I turn fragmented problems into systems people — and AI — can act on. Four areas, and what that's looked like in real projects.

01

Systems synthesis

I look for the one problem hiding inside five separately-scoped issues, then map the states, relationships, and tradeoffs underneath it. The team ends up building from one shared picture rather than five patched symptoms.

02

Decision architecture

I turn ambiguity into working distinctions, like confirmed vs. proposed, evidence vs. assumption, current vs. future. Writing the rationale down means a decision doesn't need to be re-explained from memory six months later.

03

AI-assisted workflows

I build AI-assisted evaluation and delivery workflows and run them against real cases. What I learn from each round shapes the next one. I also build the handoff structure that keeps AI coding tools working from decisions that are already made.

04

Accessible interaction craft

Full state, persistence, and error-timing models for complex components, designed accessible from the first sketch.

About

Working style

I'm a systems thinker who does my best work in the parts other people find exhausting: where what users expect, what the product actually does, and what engineering can realistically build all pull in different directions. My job is to figure out what's actually going on, get a cross-functional team aligned on it, and take the problem from a vague first conversation to a shipped outcome, then write it all down so nobody has to reverse-engineer my thinking later.
